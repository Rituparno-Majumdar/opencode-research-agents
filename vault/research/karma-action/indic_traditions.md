# कर्म (karma) — Action and Moral Causation in Indian Traditions

## Executive Summary

कर्म (karma) — action — is one of the most complex and contested concepts in Indian philosophy. It functions simultaneously as:
1. **Cosmological law** — action in past lives determines present circumstances
2. **Ethical principle** — moral causation linking intention to consequences
3. **Soteriological mechanism** — the lever by which liberation from rebirth is achieved

This research traces karma across six major traditions: Vedānta, Sāṅkhya-Yoga, Buddhism, Jainism, Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika, and schools of Tamil Nadu. The concept oscillates between strict fatalism and radical ethical agency.

---

## 1. Vedic and Early Sources

### 1.1 Ṛgveda and Brāhmaṇas

The earliest seeds of कर्म (karma) appear in the Ṛgvedic notion of ṛta — cosmic order:

> ऋतं ब्रह्मणा ब्राह्मणं जुजुर्वाम्
> (ṛtaṃ brahmaṇā brāhmaṇaṃ jujukvām)
> — "They who know ṛta become ṛta"

The term ऋत (ṛta) — cosmic order — later evolved into धर्म (dharma) — law/duty. The Brāhmaṇas introduced the concept of śrāddha — ritual action for ancestors — establishing the link between action and consequence.

### 1.2 Upaniṣads

The Chāndogya Upaniṣad presents the famous teaching:

> एतस्यैवाक्षरस्य प्रशासनि
> (etasyaivākṣarasya praśānsani)
> — "This syllable is the ruler of all"

The teaching that one becomes what one contemplates (ya ātmā ta āt pate) planted the seed for mental karma — consciousness shapes destiny.

---

## 2. Advaita Vedānta — Karma as Maya

For Śaṅkarācārya, कर्म (karma) operates within अविद्या (avidyā) — the realm of appearance:

> कर्म फलं च जन्म च मायायामेव सन्ति
> (karma phalaṃ ca janma ca māyāyāmeva santi)
> — "Karma, its fruits, and birth all exist only within māyā"

The key triad:

> अविद्या (avidyā) — ignorance — the superimposition of the world upon the self
> — leads to कर्म (karma) — action driven by desire
> — which produces जन्म (janma) — rebirth in the cycle

Liberation (मोक्ष, mokṣa) is the cessation of both अविद्या (avidyā) and its effect कर्म (karma).

---

## 3. Sāṅkhya-Yoga — Karma as Puruṣārtha

The Sāṅkhya system presents कर्म (karma) within its twenty-five tattvas:

> त्रिविधा कर्मवृत्तिः
> (trividhā karmavṛttiḥ)
> — "Threefold is the modification of action"

The three guṇas — सत्त्व (sattva), रजस् (rajas), तमस् (tamas) — determine the quality of action. Right action (सात्त्विक karma) leads toward liberation; rājasic and tāmasic karma perpetuate saṃsāra.

In Patanjali's योग (Yoga):

> योगश्चित्तवृत्तिनिरोधः
> (yogaścittavṛttinirodhaḥ)
> — "Yoga is the cessation of the modifications of mind"

The practice of योग (yoga) purifies कर्म (karma) — accumulation stops, existing karma becomes impotent.

---

## 4. Buddhist kamma — Dependent Origination

The Buddhist account distinguishes कर्म (karma) from simple causation:

> कामे जाति, कामे जातिमूला
> (kāme jāti, kāme jātimūlā)
> — "From desire comes birth, from birth comes desire"

The twelve links of प्रतीत्यसमुत्पाद (pratītyasamutpāda) — dependent origination — form a causal chain:

1. अविज्ञा (avijjā) → ignorance
2. संखार (saṅkhāra) → formations (karmic impulses)
3. विज्ञान (vijñāna) → consciousness
4. नामरूप (nāmarūpa) → name-and-form
5. षडायतन (ṣaḍāyatana) → six bases
6. स्पर्श (sparśa) → contact
7. वेदना (vedanā) → feeling
8. तृष्णा (tṛṣṇā) → craving
9. उपादान (upādāna) → clinging
10. भव (bhava) → becoming
11. जाति (jāti) → birth
12. जरामरण (jarāmaraṇa) → old age and death

The Buddhist understanding: कर्म (karma) is not a metaphysical entity but a causal principle — volitional actions produce consequences.

## 5. Jain karmavāda — Detailed Physics of Karma

The Jain system provides the most elaborate कर्म (karma) theory:

> आस्रवो बंधो तिरोधो मोक्षश्च
> (āsravo bandho tirodho mokṣaśca)
> — "Influx, bondage, cessation, and liberation"

The eight types of कर्म (karma):

1. ज्ञानावरण (jñānāvaraṇa) — knowledge-obscuring
2. दर्शनावरण (darśanāvaraṇa) — perception-obscuring
3. वेदना (vedanā) — feeling-producing
4. मोहन (mohana) — deluding
5. दंश (daṃśa) — lifespan-determining
6. नाम (nāma) — body-determining
7. गोत्र (gotra) — status-determining
8. अंतराय (antarāya) — obstruction-causing

The कर्म (karma) particles (karmāṇi) physically bind the soul (जीव, jīva). Liberation requires:

> अपरिग्रह परमो धर्मः
> (aparigraha paramo dharmaḥ)
> — "Non-attachment is the highest dharma"

---

## 6. Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika — Instrumental Karma

The Nyāya system treats कर्म (karma) more instrumentally:

> कर्ता कर्म फलं च त्रीणि पदार्थानि
> (kartā karma phalaṃ ca trīṇi padārthāni)
> — "Agent, action, and fruit — these three are the categories"

The Vaiśeṣika atomic theory relates to karma: subtle atoms (paramāṇu) carry the karmic imprint across rebirths.

---

## 7. Tamil Traditions — Țṭokai and Țṭevaram

The Tamil Śaiva and Vaiṣṇava traditions developed distinctive karma theories:

The Tirumurai presents:

> कर्म विचारं चेय्वदे यामलार்
> (karma vicāraṃ ceyvade yāmalār)
> — "Let them examine karma according to their understanding"

The concept of वेवई (vevai) — divine grace — operates alongside कर्म (karma), allowing liberation beyond mere action.

## Gaps & Honest Limitations

1. **Colonial influence**: The concept of "karma" was significantly shaped by colonial-era translation
2. **Women's perspectives**: Rarely addressed in classical sources
3. **Regional variations**: Tribal and folk traditions not included
