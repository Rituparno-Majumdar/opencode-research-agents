# Soul vs Process Divergence

The fundamental metaphysical divergence across reincarnation traditions: Does something substantial (soul, self, jīva) persist through rebirth, or is rebirth merely the continuation of a causal process without permanent entity?

## Appears In
- [[research/reincarnation/indic_traditions]] — Hindu ātman vs Buddhist anattā
- [[research/reincarnation/western_philosophy]] — Platonic soul vs process thinking

## Evidence
- Hindu traditions: ātman/jīva as eternal substance that transmigrates
- Buddhist: paṭiccasamuppāda as causal process, no ātman
- Jain: jīva as soul with karmic particles clinging
- Greek: metempsychosis of substantial soul
- Advaita: apparent soul, ultimately identical with Brahman

## Cross-Tradition Notes
This divergence cannot be resolved through reinterpretation—Hindu ātman and Buddhist anattā represent incompatible ontologies. Yet both lead to liberation: through knowledge of ātman (Hindu) or through realization of no-self (Buddhist).
