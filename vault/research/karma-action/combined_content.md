=== INDIC TRADITIONS ===

कर्म (karma) — action — is one of the most complex and contested concepts in Indian philosophy. It functions simultaneously as:
1. Cosmological law — action in past lives determines present circumstances
2. Ethical principle — moral causation linking intention to consequences
3. Soteriological mechanism — the lever by which liberation from rebirth is achieved

Key concepts:
- कर्म (karma) — action
- अविद्या (avidyā) — ignorance
- जन्म (janma) — birth
- मोक्ष (mokṣa) — liberation
- सांसार (saṃsāra) — cycle of rebirth
- प्रतीत्यसमुत्पाद (pratītyasamutpāda) — dependent origination
- धर्म (dharma) — law/duty

Buddhist kamma: 
- कामे जाति, कामे जातिमूला (kāme jāti, kāme jātimūlā) — "From desire comes birth, from birth comes desire"

Jain karmavāda:
- आस्रवो बंधो तिरोधो मोक्षश्च (āsravo bandho tirodho mokṣaśca) — "Influx, bondage, cessation, and liberation"
- Eight types of karma: ज्ञानावरण, दर्शनावरण, वेदना, मोहन, दंश, नाम, गोत्र, अंतराय

=== WESTERN PHILOSOPHY ===

Greek concepts:
- μοῖρα (moira) — fate (externally decreed)
- ἕξις (hexis) — moral habit (within-lifetime causation)
- λόγος (logos) — rational principle (immanent, like karma)

Stoic determinism:
- Prohairesis (choice) vs external fate
- Similar to karma: internal intent vs external results

Kant: moral law is categorical — consequence irrelevant (contrast to karma)
Schopenhauer: Will-to-live drives rebirth (similar to saṃsāra)

=== ANCIENT CIVILIZATIONS ===

Egyptian:
- मा'त (ma'at) — cosmic order, but within single lifetime, not rebirth

Mesopotamian:
- ṣīmtu (fate) — externally decreed by gods

Chinese:
- 天命 (tiānmìng) — mandate of heaven
- 德 (dé) — virtue affects fate, but no rebirth mechanism

Key distinction:
- Indic karma = internal generation + rebirth
- Greek/Chinese = external decree or linear life only

=== CONTEMPORARY SCHOLARSHIP ===

- Wendy Doniger: karma = action primarily, not consequence
- Ian Stevenson: reincarnation research (controversial)
- Cognitive science: karma as "minimally counterintuitive" concept
- Popular usage: karma as immediate retributive justice (different from classical)
