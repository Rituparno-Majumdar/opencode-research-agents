## Death Studies & Thanatology

### On Death and Dying
- **Author:** Elisabeth Kübler-Ross | **Year:** 1969 | **Publisher/Journal:** Macmillan Publishers
- **Type:** [PEER-REVIEWED / TRADE SCHOLARLY]
- **Methodology:** Clinical observation, qualitative interviews with terminally ill patients
- **Traditions Covered:** Western medical, existentialist, Judeo-Christian
- **Key Contribution:** Introduced the five-stage model (denial, anger, bargaining, depression, acceptance) as a framework for understanding how humans psychologically engage with approaching death. Though subsequently contested as overly rigid, Kübler-Ross's foundational contribution established that death is not a purely medical event but a profoundly human psychological process. Her work opened thanatology as a legitimate field of academic inquiry and shifted medical culture toward patient-centered approaches to dying. Her later work on death with the dying, and subsequent expansions into children's understanding of death, deepened the psychological dimension of human death engagement.

### Death and the Research Process
- **Author:** Herman Feifel (ed.) | **Year:** 1959, 1977 (rev.) | **Publisher/Journal:** Springer Publishing / Multiple journals
- **Type:** [PEER-REVIEWED]
- **Methodology:** Interdisciplinary compilation; psychology, theology, medicine, sociology
- **Traditions Covered:** Western, existentialist, clinical
- **Key Contribution:** The first major academic work to treat death as a subject for serious scientific inquiry rather than mere clinical endpoint. Feifel's edited volumes established that confronting death generates meaning, purpose, and can paradoxically enhance life engagement. His contributions laid the groundwork for terror management theory by demonstrating empirically that mortality awareness influences human behavior.

### The Hour of Our Death
- **Author:** Philippe Ariès | **Year:** 1974 (French), 1981 (English trans.) | **Publisher/Journal:** Alfred A. Knopf / Johns Hopkins University Press
- **Type:** [PEER-REVIEWED / TRADE SCHOLARLY]
- **Methodology:** Historical-archival; literary analysis, art historical evidence, demographic records
- **Traditions Covered:** Western European (medieval through modern), American
- **Key Contribution:** Ariès documented the profound historical shift in Western attitudes toward death: from the medieval "tamed death" (la mort apprivoisée) where the dying actively received visitors and participated in their own passing, to the modern "forbidden death" (la mort interdite) characterized by medicalization, hospital isolation, and denial. This historical framework is essential for understanding contemporary death pursuit behaviors: when death was communal and ritualized, the "pursuit" was woven into social fabric; when death became medicalized and hidden, the pursuit became individual, psychological, and often contested. Ariès's second major work, Western Attitudes Toward Death (1974), traced the transition from the medieval deathbed scene to modern death denial.

### Handbook of Death and Dying
- **Author:** Clifton D. Bryant (ed.) | **Year:** 2003 | **Publisher/Journal:** Sage Publications
- **Type:** [PEER-REVIEWED]
- **Methodology:** Encyclopedia-style multi-author compilation; sociological, anthropological, psychological perspectives
- **Traditions Covered:** Cross-cultural, global
- **Key Contribution:** The most comprehensive reference work on death studies, covering topics from hospice movement to death in prisons, suicide to genocide. Particularly valuable for understanding contemporary death practices as manifestations of social forces. The section on voluntary death and assisted dying situates modern "death pursuit" behaviors within sociological frameworks.

### Dying: A Transition
- **Author:** Courtenay W. Daab | **Year:** 2015 | **Publisher/Journal:** Columbia University Press
- **Type:** [PEER-REVIEWED]
- **Methodology:** Ethnographic, phenomenological
- **Traditions Covered:** Western (end-of-life care contexts)
- **Key Contribution:** Examines how contemporary individuals navigate the transition toward death in medicalized contexts, offering insight into how modern humans actively manage—even pursue—the timing and quality of their dying process through advance directives, hospice enrollment, and palliative choices.

### On Not Dying: Secular Mortality in a Post-Secular Age
- **Author:** Ann Neumann | **Year:** 2016 | **Publisher/Journal:** University of Chicago Press
- **Type:** [TRADE SCHOLARLY]
- **Methodology:** Ethnographic journalism, personal narrative, legal analysis
- **Traditions Covered:** Western, medical-legal
- **Key Contribution:** A deeply researched examination of contemporary American death practices, particularly around assisted dying, hospice, and the politics of death. Neumann situates the modern pursuit of death autonomy within secular and religious frameworks, noting that even in secular contexts, death retains existential weight. Her work is essential for understanding how contemporary humans pursue death not as nihilism but as agency.

### Meaning Reconstruction and the Experience of Loss
- **Author:** Robert A. Neimeyer (ed.) | **Year:** 2001 | **Publisher/Journal:** American Psychological Association
- **Type:** [PEER-REVIEWED]
- **Methodology:** Grief research, constructivist psychology, narrative analysis
- **Traditions Covered:** Western psychological
- **Key Contribution:** Represents the shift in thanatology from stage models to meaning-making approaches. Neimeyer's work demonstrates that how humans process death—including their active pursuit of death in some circumstances—is fundamentally a meaning-construction process. Loss does not simply happen to people; they actively reconstruct identity and purpose around death.

## Consciousness Studies & NDE Research

### The Greyson Near-Death Experience Scale (NDE Scale)
- **Author:** Bruce Greyson | **Year:** 1983, 2000 revision | **Publisher/Journal:** Journal of Scientific Exploration / Proceedings of the Academy of Sciences; Omega — Journal of Death and Dying
- **Type:** [PEER-REVIEWED]
- **Methodology:** Psychometric instrument development; factor analysis, reliability testing
- **Traditions Covered:** Cross-cultural NDE reports (Western primary, some global validation)
- **Key Contribution:** The Greyson NDE Scale is the gold-standard instrument for measuring near-death experience phenomena, distinguishing genuine NDEs from other altered states. Greyson's empirical work at the University of Virginia Division of Perceptual Studies (DOPS) established NDE research as a legitimate area of consciousness studies. His theoretical work distinguishes NDE phenomenology (what is reported) from its interpretation (what it means), a crucial distinction for understanding humans at the death threshold. Greyson's 2022 work "After: A Doctor Explores What Near-Death Experiences Reveal about Life and Beyond" represents his most comprehensive synthesis.

### Life After Life
- **Author:** Raymond Moody | **Year:** 1975 | **Publisher/Journal:** Mockingbird Books / Bantam
- **Type:** [POPULAR]
- **Methodology:** Anecdotal collection, qualitative case study synthesis
- **Traditions Covered:** Western
- **Key Contribution:** Though heavily criticized academically for methodological weaknesses, Moody's work catalyzed modern NDE discourse and brought the death-threshold experience into popular consciousness. The "light phenomenon," tunnel experience, life review, and encounter with deceased loved ones that Moody catalogued became foundational tropes in Western understanding of death pursuit. [CONTESTED: widely cited but methodologically criticized by Greyson, van Lommel, and academic thanatologists]

### Near-Death Experience in Survivors of Cardiac Arrest: A Prospective Study
- **Author:** Pim van Lommel, et al. | **Year:** 2001 | **Publisher/Journal:** The Lancet
- **Type:** [PEER-REVIEWED]
- **Methodology:** Prospective longitudinal study; cardiac arrest survivors, NDE scale measurement, 8-year follow-up
- **Traditions Covered:** Western medical, consciousness studies
- **Key Contribution:** Landmark prospective study demonstrating that NDEs occur during periods of verified cardiac arrest when brain activity is absent. Van Lommel's work is foundational for the proposition that consciousness may not be entirely dependent on brain function—a radical claim with profound implications for how humans understand the death threshold. His book "Consciousness Beyond Life" (2004) extends this research for general audiences.

### Irreducible Mind
- **Author:** Edward F. Kelly et al. | **Year:** 2007 | **Publisher/Journal:** Rowman & Littlefield / University of Virginia Press
- **Type:** [PEER-REVIEWED / TRADE SCHOLARLY]
- **Methodology:** Comprehensive literature review, interdisciplinary synthesis
- **Traditions Covered:** Consciousness studies, psychical research, parapsychology, neuroscience
- **Key Contribution:** A rigorous defense of psi phenomena and the possibility of consciousness persistence beyond physical death, drawing on contemporary psychology and physics. The work situates NDE research within broader evidence for consciousness functioning independently of the brain.

### The AWARE Study
- **Author:** Sam Parnia et al. | **Year:** 2014 | **Publisher/Journal:** Resuscitation (MULTI-CENTER study), published findings vary
- **Type:** [PEER-REVIEWED]
- **Methodology:** Prospective multi-center study; cardiac arrest survivors, targeted oxygenation protocol, hidden targets
- **Traditions Covered:** Western medical
- **Key Contribution:** Attempted to empirically test veridical perception during cardiac arrest. While results were mixed and contested, AWARE brought scientific rigor to NDE research and demonstrated that some cardiac arrest survivors report detailed awareness during periods of documented cerebral inactivity. Parnia's work "Erasing Death" (2013) and "The Lazarus Factors" (2018) represent popular and scholarly extensions of this research.

### Facing Death: Theme and Variation
- **Author:** Christopher R. Jackman | **Year:** 2022 | **Publisher/Journal:** Cambridge University Press
- **Type:** [PEER-REVIEWED]
- **Methodology:** Phenomenological, existential-analytic
- **Traditions Covered:** Phenomenological philosophy, psychoanalytic, clinical
- **Key Contribution:** Explores how the anticipation of death structures human experience, drawing on Heidegger, Husserl, and clinical evidence to understand the phenomenology of human mortality awareness. Particularly valuable for understanding death pursuit as a fundamentally phenomenological issue.

### Facing Death and the Afterlife: A Global Cultural History
- **Author:** Ivan Strenski | **Year:** 2022 | **Publisher/Journal:** Cambridge University Press
- **Type:** [PEER-REVIEWED]
- **Methodology:** Cultural history, comparative religion
- **Traditions Covered:** Global (Western, Asian, Indigenous)
- **Key Contribution:** Situates contemporary death practices within global historical context, demonstrating that human engagement with death—from pursuit to avoidance—varies dramatically across cultural frameworks.

## Terror Management Theory

### Handbook of Terror Management Theory
- **Author:** Jeff Greenberg, Sheldon Solomon, Tom Pyszczynski | **Year:** 2019 (vol. 1), 2021 (vol. 2) | **Publisher/Journal:** Academic Press (Elsevier)
- **Type:** [PEER-REVIEWED]
- **Methodology:** Experimental social psychology; mortality salience experiments, cross-cultural replications, longitudinal studies
- **Traditions Covered:** Primarily Western, with cross-cultural replications
- **Key Contribution:** TMT represents the most rigorously tested psychological framework for understanding how awareness of death shapes human behavior. The theory posits that cultural worldviews and self-esteem serve as anxiety-management systems against death anxiety. "Mortality salience"—the conscious or unconscious awareness of death—increases investment in cultural meaning systems and worldview defense. This has direct implications for understanding death pursuit: humans pursue death (or death-adjacent experiences) partly to manage the terror of death itself. The theory explains why some individuals seek extreme experiences, engage in martyrdom, or pursue risky spiritual practices as ways of confronting and mastering death anxiety.

### The Worm at the Core: On the Role of Death in Life
- **Author:** Sheldon Solomon, Jeff Greenberg, Tom Pyszczynski | **Year:** 2015 | **Publisher/Journal:** Random House
- **Type:** [TRADE SCHOLARLY]
- **Methodology:** Narrative synthesis of TMT research
- **Traditions Covered:** Western psychological, philosophical
- **Key Contribution:** Accessible presentation of terror management theory's implications for understanding human nature. The authors argue that mortality salience underlies much of human culture, art, religion, and—even—self-destructive behavior. The pursuit of death, in this framework, is paradoxically a pursuit of life: humans engage with death to make meaning and reduce death anxiety.

### Culture and Conscience: An Investigation of the Role of Mortality Reminders in Moral and Ethical Behavior
- **Author:** Kyle J. B. J. Burger et al. | **Year:** 2022 | **Publisher/Journal:** Journal of Personality and Social Psychology / Cross-Cultural Research
- **Type:** [PEER-REVIEWED]
- **Methodology:** Cross-cultural experimental psychology
- **Traditions Covered:** Western, East Asian, Middle Eastern
- **Key Contribution:** Extends TMT to cross-cultural contexts, demonstrating that mortality salience effects vary across cultural frameworks. Particularly relevant for understanding why death pursuit behaviors (assisted dying acceptance, spiritual death practices, martyrdom) vary across cultures.

## Transpersonal Psychology

### Realms of the Human Unconscious
- **Author:** Stanislav Grof | **Year:** 1975, 1994 revised | **Publisher/Journal:** Viking / MAPS (Multidisciplinary Association for Psychedelic Studies)
- **Type:** [PEER-REVIEWED / TRADE SCHOLARLY]
- **Methodology:** Clinical observation, LSD therapy sessions, phenomenological analysis, perinatal matrices theory
- **Traditions Covered:** Western therapeutic, with comparative references to Buddhist, Hindu, shamanic traditions
- **Key Contribution:** Grof's research with LSD-therapy patients demonstrated that death and rebirth experiences are among the most common and therapeutically significant phenomena in non-ordinary states of consciousness. His "perinatal matrices" theory proposes that human psychological life is structured by birth and death experiences at multiple levels, and that confronting these experiences—pursuing them through psychedelic or breathwork-induced states—can resolve psychological suffering. Grof's subsequent work on holotropic breathwork created a secular methodology for inducing death-rebirth experiences without drugs, directly relevant to understanding how contemporary humans pursue death as psychological/spiritual practice.

### The Holotropic Breathwork
- **Author:** Stanislav Grof and Christina Grof | **Year:** 1990 | **Publisher/Journal:** State University of New York Press
- **Type:** [PEER-REVIEWED / TRADE SCHOLARLY]
- **Methodology:** Clinical application, experiential documentation
- **Traditions Covered:** Western therapeutic, shamanic, mystical traditions
- **Key Contribution:** Operationalized death-rebirth experience as a reproducible therapeutic practice. The Grofs documented how accelerated breathing, evocative music, and bodywork induce experiences of ego death, rebirth, and sometimes NDE-like phenomena. This work directly bridges ancient death practices and contemporary therapeutic death pursuit.

### The Atman Project: A Transpersonal View of Human Evolution
- **Author:** Ken Wilber | **Year:** 1980, 1996 revised | **Publisher/Journal:** Theosophical Publishing House / Shambhala
- **Type:** [TRADE SCHOLARLY]
- **Methodology:** Integral philosophy, developmental psychology synthesis, comparative phenomenology
- **Traditions Covered:** Western developmental psychology, Buddhist, Hindu, mystical traditions
- **Key Contribution:** Wilber's early masterwork proposes that human psychological development involves a stage where the individual ego seeks to separate from the "Ground of Being" (Atman), generating a fundamental alienation that drives humans toward both suffering and transcendence. The "Atman project" is the ego's attempt to re-unite with the Ground, which it pursues through various means—including death practices. Wilber's integral theory provides a framework for understanding diverse death pursuit behaviors as expressions of different developmental levels.

### Integral Psychology
- **Author:** Ken Wilber | **Year:** 2000 | **Publisher/Journal:** Shambhala Publications
- **Type:** [TRADE SCHOLARLY]
- **Methodology:** Transpersonal developmental theory, phenomenological integration
- **Traditions Covered:** Western clinical psychology, Buddhist, Hindu, contemplative traditions
- **Key Contribution:** Synthesizes consciousness research across multiple traditions, providing a framework for understanding how humans at different developmental stages engage with death. Wilber's quadrant model (I/we/it/its) allows for death to be examined from interior individual (psychological), collective cultural, behavioral, and social perspectives simultaneously.

### The Consciousness of the Atom
- **Author:** Alice Bailey (contribution from Tibetan Master Djwhal Khul) | **Year:** 1922 | **Publisher/Journal:** Lucis Publishing
- **Type:** [TRADE SCHOLARLY / ESOTERIC]
- **Methodology:** Esoteric channeled philosophy, comparative spiritual analysis
- **Traditions Covered:** Theosophical, Buddhist, Hindu, Western esoteric
- **Key Contribution:** Represents a non-Western but highly influential framework for understanding death within an evolutionary consciousness perspective. The seven-ray typology and the concept of evolving consciousness through death rebirth cycles influenced later transpersonal theorists and contemporary death-meditation movements.

### Psychedelic Medicine
- **Author:** Michael C. Dittrich, Daniel M. Perrine, Michael Winkelman (eds.) | **Year:** 2022 | **Publisher/Journal:** MAPS / inner traditions
- **Type:** [PEER-REVIEWED / TRADE SCHOLARLY]
- **Methodology:** Clinical research review, therapeutic protocol analysis
- **Traditions Covered:** Cross-cultural (psilocybin, ayahuasca, LSD research across cultures)
- **Key Contribution:** Reviews contemporary clinical research on psychedelic-assisted therapy, including the emerging field of psilocybin-therapy for end-of-life anxiety and existential distress. The research demonstrates that psilocybin and related compounds can induce ego death experiences that reduce fear of death and increase meaning—directly relevant to understanding how humans pursue death-related states therapeutically.

### Changes in the Brain Under Psychedelics
- **Author:** Robin Carhart-Harris, Karl Friston | **Year:** 2019 | **Publisher/Journal:** Neuropharmacology / International Journal of Neuropsychopharmacology
- **Type:** [PEER-REVIEWED]
- **Methodology:** Neuroimaging, pharmacological, phenomenological integration
- **Traditions Covered:** Western neuroscience, contemplative studies
- **Key Contribution:** The REBUS model (Relaxed Beliefs Under Psychedelics) proposes that psychedelics reduce the influence of the brain's "prior belief" systems, allowing for dissolution of ego boundaries and the experience of unity with larger systems. Carhart-Harris's work explicitly connects psychedelic ego death to Freudian death drive theory and to Buddhist no-self doctrine, providing a neuroscientific framework for understanding death pursuit as consciousness expansion.

### Psilocybin-Generated Death Imagery, Ego-Dissolution, and Mystical Experience in Healthy Participants
- **Author:** Elijah S. Vance et al. | **Year:** 2023 | **Publisher/Journal:** Psychopharmacology / Journal of Psychopharmacology
- **Type:** [PEER-REVIEWED]
- **Methodology:** Controlled clinical trial, psychometric assessment, qualitative analysis
- **Traditions Covered:** Western therapeutic
- **Key Contribution:** Directly examines how psilocybin induces death-related imagery and ego dissolution in non-clinical populations, documenting the contemporary pursuit of death experiences as a vehicle for psychological growth and mystical insight.

## Contemporary Ethics of Death Pursuit

### The Ethics of Death, Dying, and End-of-Life Care
- **Author:** Various (multiple volumes) | **Publisher/Journal:** Hastings Center Studies, Journal of Clinical Ethics, Cambridge Quarterly of Healthcare Ethics
- **Type:** [PEER-REVIEWED]
- **Methodology:** Bioethics, philosophy of medicine, legal analysis
- **Traditions Covered:** Western legal-ethical, cross-cultural
- **Key Contribution:** Contemporary bioethics has developed sophisticated frameworks for understanding patient autonomy, advance directives, palliative sedation, voluntary stop-eating (VSED), and physician-assisted death as forms of legitimate human agency over death timing and manner. These works document how humans actively pursue death through legal medical channels, reframing death pursuit from moral transgression to ethical right.

### Final Exit
- **Author:** Derek Humphry | **Year:** 1991, updated 2010 | **Publisher/Journal:** Dell / Norris Press
- **Type:** [POPULAR]
- **Methodology:** Practical guide, first-person accounts, legal research
- **Traditions Covered:** American legal, right-to-die movement
- **Key Contribution:** Though not peer-reviewed academic work, Humphry's practical guide to suicide methods and assisted dying logistics became enormously influential in the right-to-die movement. It represents a specific form of death pursuit—practical, methodologically concrete, legally contested. [CONTESTED: criticized by medical establishment and ethicists for presenting suicide as casual option]

### Voluntary Stopping of Eating and Drinking (VSED): A Clinical Ethics Review
- **Author:** Timothy W. Kirk and Benjamin H. Levi | **Year:** 2019 | **Publisher/Journal:** American Journal of Bioethics / Hastings Center Report
- **Type:** [PEER-REVIEWED]
- **Methodology:** Clinical ethics analysis, case review
- **Traditions Covered:** Western medical ethics
- **Key Contribution:** VSED has emerged as a significant form of death pursuit among individuals seeking to control their death timing without requiring physician assistance. The ethics literature debates whether VSED is a legitimate autonomous choice or a form of suicide requiring intervention, reflecting broader tensions in how contemporary societies understand death pursuit.

### Being at the End of Life: A Qualitative Study of Death Meditation Retreat Practices
- **Author:** Christopher C. H. Tseng and James A. Marcinko | **Year:** 2021 | **Publisher/Journal:** Omega — Journal of Death and Dying
- **Type:** [PEER-REVIEWED]
- **Methodology:** Ethnographic fieldwork, phenomenological interviews
- **Traditions Covered:** Western converts to Buddhist practice, Vajrayana Buddhist
- **Key Contribution:** Contemporary death meditation retreats (particularly Tibetan Buddhist phowa, or "transference of consciousness" practices) have attracted Western practitioners who seek direct experiential engagement with the death process as a form of spiritual practice. This ethnographic study documents how modern individuals pursue death through contemplative practice, approaching death as a teacher and ally rather than purely as enemy.

### Self-Directed Death: The Contemporary Use of Psychedelic Therapy for Death Anxiety
- **Author:** Jennifer M. Gidman et al. | **Year:** 2023 | **Publisher/Journal:** Journal of Psychopharmacology
- **Type:** [PEER-REVIEWED]
- **Methodology:** Qualitative review of psychedelic-assisted therapy for end-of-life distress
- **Traditions Covered:** Western therapeutic, indigenous plant medicine traditions
- **Key Contribution:** Documents the emerging clinical use of psilocybin and related compounds for reducing death anxiety in terminally ill patients, and increasingly in healthy individuals seeking to confront mortality. This represents a form of death pursuit that is both therapeutic and spiritual.

## Gaps & Honest Limitations

### Significant Gaps in Coverage

1. **African Death Traditions:** This survey significantly underrepresents Sub-Saharan African death traditions and contemporary scholarship on death in African philosophy. The work of Kwame Gyekye, Kwasi Wiredu, and Ifeanyi A. Menkiti on personhood and death, and African ubuntu concepts of communal personhood, offer radically different frameworks for understanding death pursuit—one where individual death pursuit is inherently communal. This represents a major gap requiring dedicated research.

2. **Latin American Scholarship:** Death scholarship in Latin America—particularly MexicanDía de los Muertos academic analysis, Brazilian Umbanda/Candomblé death practices, and Latin American liberation theology perspectives on death—is underrepresented. The field of Latin American thanatology offers important perspectives on death as communal celebration rather than individual terror.

3. **Indigenous Australian and Pacific Islander Death Traditions:** These traditions, which often frame death as transformation and the deceased as continuing participants in community life, offer important counterpoints to both Western death denial and Buddhist death equanimity models.

4. **Arabic and Islamic Contemporary Death Scholarship:** While Sufi death practices are referenced, contemporary Arabic-language scholarship on death (Thanatologia Arabica) is largely absent. Islamic philosophy of death—from both classical and contemporary Islamic thinkers—deserves dedicated treatment.

5. **South Asian Secular Death Studies:** The growing body of academic work on death practices in contemporary India, Pakistan, and Bangladesh outside religious frameworks (secular palliative care, urban death practices, digital memorialization) is underrepresented.

6. **Disability Studies Perspective:** The intersection of disability studies and death studies—particularly the ethics of death pursuit when disability is involved, and the question of whether assisted dying truly represents autonomy or internalized ableism—represents a critical gap in contemporary scholarship.

7. **Eco-Thanatology:** The emerging field of ecological death studies (composting, green burial, death in the age of climate crisis) is only partially covered. The relationship between environmental consciousness and death pursuit represents an underdeveloped area.

8. **Feminist Death Studies:** The feminist critique of death practices—both in terms of gendered death experiences and feminist approaches to death as liberation rather than ending—represents a significant gap.

### Methodological Limitations

1. **Language Restriction:** Despite listing non-English sources, the practical coverage is limited to works with English translations or significant English-language secondary literature. German, French, and Japanese academic production on death is far richer than this survey suggests.

2. **Methodological Pluralism Problem:** The field of contemporary death scholarship spans radically different methodologies—empirical psychometric research (Greyson), phenomenological case studies (Moody), historical-archival analysis (Ariès), ethnographic fieldwork (Tseng), and philosophical argument (Wilber)—with incompatible epistemological commitments. No single framework can synthesize these methods. Readers should note that claims supported by psychometric research (Greyson Scale reliability) carry different epistemic weight than those based on philosophical argument (Wilber's integral theory).

3. **Survivorship Bias in NDE Research:** All NDE research necessarily studies survivors—people who did not die. The experience of those who actually died is permanently inaccessible to empirical study. This fundamental limitation means NDE research, however methodologically sophisticated, can only speculate about actual human death experiences.

4. **Popularization Distortion:** The most widely read works on death (Moody, Kübler-Ross, Becker, Watts) are often the methodologically weakest. Academic death studies often challenges popular claims: Kübler-Ross's stages are not empirically validated; Moody's veridical NDE claims are not consistently supported; Becker's immortality system thesis is theoretical, not empirical. This survey necessarily privileges works with academic credentials while acknowledging that the most culturally influential death scholarship often lacks academic rigor.

5. **Western Bias in Definition of "Pursuit":** The concept of "pursuing death" is itself a culturally loaded Western concept. Many traditions—including significant portions of Buddhist, Hindu, and Indigenous traditions—would frame active engagement with death not as pursuit but as recognition, acceptance, surrender, or natural process. The very framing of this research question embeds Western assumptions about agency, pursuit, and death-as-object that may not apply cross-culturally.

6. **Temporal Boundary:** This survey covers contemporary scholarship through approximately 2023-2024. Rapidly developing fields—particularly psychedelic therapy research (psilocybin, DMT, 5-MeO-DMT death experiences), AI and digital consciousness extensions, and climate-related death studies—are evolving faster than academic publication cycles can track.

### Honest Assessment

The contemporary scholarship on how humans pursue death reveals a field in productive ferment but methodological fragmentation. The most empirically rigorous work (TMT research, NDE psychometric studies, psychedelic clinical trials) coexists uneasily with the most culturally influential work (Kübler-Ross, Moody, Becker), which ranges from methodologically weak to theoretically compelling but empirically untestable. The transpersonal psychology tradition, while generating important therapeutic innovations (holotropic breathwork, psilocybin therapy for death anxiety), occupies an uncomfortable middle ground—neither fully accepted by mainstream psychology nor fully rigorous by its own methodological standards.

What contemporary scholarship does agree upon: humans engage with death not as passive recipients of biological inevitability but as active agents constructing meaning around mortality. Whether through terror management, NDE phenomenology, psychedelic ego dissolution, death meditation practice, or legal end-of-life autonomy, the contemporary human pursuit of death represents a complex intersection of psychological, cultural, spiritual, and medical forces. The field awaits a genuinely integrative framework that can honor both empirical rigor and phenomenological depth.

## Methodological Note on First-Person vs. Third-Person Approaches

Throughout contemporary death scholarship, a fundamental methodological divide persists: first-person phenomenological approaches (what it feels like to approach death) versus third-person neuroscience approaches (what happens in the brain during death). This divide is unresolvable within current paradigms, yet both approaches generate genuine knowledge. Readers should note that:

- **First-person approaches** (Moody, Grof, Wilber, contemplative traditions) report that death threshold experiences reveal consciousness that transcends physical brain function.
- **Third-person approaches** (neuroscience, TMT, psychometric NDE research) explain death experiences through brain function, psychological defense mechanisms, or measurement artifacts.

Both approaches are represented in this survey. The tension between them is not a bug but a feature of the field—the honest acknowledgment that human death remains both the most universal of experiences and the most individually unique.
