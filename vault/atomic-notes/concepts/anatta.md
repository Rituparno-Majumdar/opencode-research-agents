# Anattā

Anattā (Pali; Sanskrit: anātman) is the Buddhist doctrine of non-self or no-soul. The Buddha explicitly rejected the existence of a permanent, substantial self (ātman), replacing it with the doctrine of dependent origination (paṭiccasamuppāda). What "rebirths" in Buddhism is a causal process, not a substantial soul.

## Cross-Tradition Notes
Anattā represents the sharpest divergence from Hindu thought. Where Hindu traditions preserve ātman as the eternal self that transmigrates, Buddhism denies any persistent entity—what continues is a process of conditional causation.
