## 2. Notable Convergences

- **Plato's Myth of Er and Indic Karma**: Both establish moral causation across multiple lives. (Indic: karma determines rebirth; Western: soul chooses next life based on previous moral choices)
- **Orphic metempsychosis and Indic samsara**: Both propose cyclic rebirth across many lives, though with different metaphysical frameworks. (Noted in ancient_civilizations and indic)
- **Schopenhauer's Will and Vedanta's Maya**: Both describe cosmic illusion/force binding individual to suffering and cyclical existence. (Noted in western_philosophy)
- **Jung's collective unconscious and Vedanta's citta**: Both suggest deep structures underlying consciousness that manifest as death-rebirth motifs. (Noted in western_philosophy)
- **Process philosophy (Whitehead) and Buddhist Pratityasamutpada**: Both reject substance metaphysics in favor of relational/process ontology. (Noted in western_philosophy)
- **Van Lommel's conclusions and Eastern consciousness views**: Both suggest consciousness persists beyond brain, but from different starting points. (Noted in contemporary)

## 3. Contradictions & Tensions

**[FACTUAL CONFLICT]**
- **Egyptian linear afterlife vs. Orphic/Greek cyclic rebirth**: Egyptian tradition presents one journey to underworld with judgment; Orphic tradition proposes multiple cycles of rebirth. Both are ancient Mediterranean but fundamentally different models. (Ancient Civilizations explicitly notes this)

**[INTERPRETIVE DIFFERENCE]**
- **Advaita Vedanta vs. Buddhist anatta**: Indic traditions within same cultural area disagree on existence of eternal self. Advaita posits Atman (eternal self); Buddhist anatta (no-self) rejects this. (Both in Indic)
- **Sufi "return to source" vs. Indic samsara**: Terminology overlaps ("return," "cycle") but Sufi metaphysics is different — return to God rather than rebirth in material world. (Ancient civilizations flags this)

**[SCOPE DISAGREEMENT]**
- **Mainstream Islam vs. Sufi interpretation**: Islamic mainstream teaches single life + resurrection (no reincarnation); Sufi traditions have more complex language that some interpret as cyclical. (Ancient civilizations distinguishes these)
- **Kabbalistic gilgul (reincarnation) vs. normative Judaism**: Kabbalah (medieval) introduces reincarnation as doctrine; normative/ rabbinical Judaism does not hold reincarnation as core belief. (Ancient civilizations notes this is late/exceptional)
