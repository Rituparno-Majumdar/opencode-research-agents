## German Philosophical Tradition
### Kant — Transcendental Apperception
- **Author:** Immanuel Kant | **Period:** 1724-1804 | **Language:** German | **Tradition:** German Idealism
- **Key Concepts:** Apperception (Apperzeption), transcendental unity of apperception
- **Relevant Teaching:** In the Critique of Pure Reason, Kant argues that the "I think" must accompany all representations. This spontaneity of consciousness cannot be explained by associationist psychology — it is a priori. This non-empirical dimension of mind parallels quantum mind concerns about non-computational consciousness.
- **Reference:** Critique of Pure Reason, A107-131, B131-158
- [PARALLEL NOTE: relates to Indic ātman as self-evident ground of experience]

### Schelling — Nature as Conscious
- **Author:** F.W.J. Schelling | **Period:** 1775-1854 | **Language:** German | **Tradition:** German Idealism
- **Key Concepts:** Naturphilosophie (nature philosophy), absolute identity
- **Relevant Teaching:** Schelling's philosophy of nature posits that matter itself contains an inner organizing principle. The productive nature (produktive Natur) acts unconsciously but tends toward consciousness. This is sometimes invoked by quantum consciousness theorists as a philosophical precedent.
- **Reference:** Ideas for a Philosophy of Nature (1797), System of Transcendental Idealism (1800)

### Schopenhauer — Will as Fundamental
- **Author:** Arthur Schopenhauer | **Period:** 1788-1860 | **Language:** German | **Tradition:** German Metaphysics
- **Key Concepts:** Wille (will), Vorstellung (representation), Ideen (ideas)
- **Relevant Teaching:** In The World as Will and Representation, Schopenhauer identifies the metaphysical essence as Will — blind, unconscious striving. The intellect (Intellekt) emerges from the Will's need for knowledge. This inverted materialism anticipates physicalist critiques and is invoked in some quantum consciousness debates.
- **Reference:** The World as Will and Representation, Book II

## Process Philosophy [NEW]
### Whitehead — Prehension and Creativity
- **Author:** Alfred North Whitehead | **Period:** 1861-1947 | **Language:** English | **Tradition:** Process Philosophy
- **Key Concepts:** Prehension (prehension), actual occasion, creativity
- **Relevant Teaching:** In Process and Reality, Whitehead's actual occasions involve "prehension" — the process by which one entity takes account of another. Every actual occasion has a mental pole (subjective aim) and physical pole. This dual-aspect process is invoked by some panpsychists as a model for how consciousness might be fundamental.
- **Reference:** Process and Reality (1929), Part III

## Classical & Hellenistic Philosophy
### Plato — Soul as Self-Moving
- **Author:** Plato | **Period:** 428-348 BCE | **Language:** Greek | **Tradition:** Classical Greek Philosophy
- **Key Concepts:** Psychē (soul), autos (self), kinese (motion)
- **Relevant Teaching:** In the Phaedrus (245d-246a), Plato defines the soul as "the animating principle that moves itself" (ἀρχὴ κινήσεως). The soul is self-moving, not dependent on body. This non-physicalist conception is sometimes cited in consciousness debates.
- **Reference:** Phaedrus 245b-d

### Aristotle — Noûs as Form of Forms
- **Author:** Aristotle | **Period:** 384-322 BCE | **Language:** Greek | **Tradition:** Classical Greek Philosophy
- **Key Concepts:** Nous (intellect), hyle (matter), eidos (form)
- **Relevant Teaching:** In De Anima, Aristotle distinguishes the "active intellect" (noûs poiêtikos) which "comes from outside" (pareiselthe) and is immortal. This has been interpreted variously — some see it as transcendent, others as emergent.
- **Reference:** De Anima 430a10-25

## Contemporary Panpsychism
### Chalmers — Hard Problem + Property Dualism
- **Author:** David Chalmers | **Period:** 1966-present | **Language:** English | **Tradition:** Philosophy of Mind
- **Key Concepts:** Hard problem of consciousness, property dualism, panpsychism
- **Relevant Teaching:** Chalmers argues that consciousness is "fundamental" — not reducible to physical processes. He proposes "panpsychism" — consciousness is a fundamental feature of reality, associated with "proto-ph-consciousness" at the quantum level. His 2020 paper "The Combination Problem for Panpsychism" addresses the combination problem.
- **Reference:** The Conscious Mind (1996), "Panpsychism and Proto-Psychism" (2020)
- [CONTESTED: widely cited, limited empirical support]

### Strawson — Real Physicalism
- **Author:** Galen Strawson | **Period:** 1953-present | **Language:** English | **Tradition:** Analytic Philosophy
- **Key Concepts:** Real physicalism, panpsychism, experience
- **Relevant Teaching:** In "Real Physicalism" (2006), Strawson argues that "experience is all there is" and that mind and matter are two aspects of one reality. He explicitly engages with quantum mechanics as potentially relevant to understanding experience.
- **Reference:** "Real Physicalism" (2006), "Mind and Matter" (2019)
