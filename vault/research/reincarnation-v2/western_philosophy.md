_Agent: Western Philosophy & Science | Run: 2026-05-19_

## German Philosophical Tradition

### Schopenhauer - Die Welt als Wille und Vorstellung (The World as Will and Representation, 1818)
- **Author:** Arthur Schopenhauer | **Period:** 19th century | **Language:** German | **Tradition:** German Idealism / Metaphysics
- **Key Concepts:** [Wille (Will)], [Vorstellung (Representation)], [Erlösung (Redemption/Liberation)]
- **Relevant Teaching:** The world is representation of Will; individual will is bound to suffering and the cycle of death and rebirth (metempsychosis). Liberation through negation of will.
- **Reference:** Book IV, §§ 68-71
- [PARALLEL NOTE: relates to Indic concept of moksha through negation]

## Cartesian Tradition

### Descartes - Meditationes de Prima Philosophia (Meditations on First Philosophy, 1641)
- **Author:** René Descartes | **Period:** 17th century | **Language:** Latin | **Tradition:** Rationalism / Substance Dualism
- **Key Concepts:** [Res cogitans (thinking substance)], [Res extensa (extended substance)], [God (nota)]
- **Relevant Teaching:** Mind-body dualism; soul as thinking substance distinct from body. Does not explicitly discuss reincarnation but provides metaphysical basis for soul survival after death.
- **Reference:** Meditations II, VI
- [PARALLEL NOTE: Different from Vedantic non-dualism; supports separate soul concept]

## Process Philosophy

### Whitehead - Process and Reality (1929)
- **Author:** Alfred North Whitehead | **Period:** 20th century | **Language:** English | **Tradition:** Process Philosophy
- **Key Concepts:** [Prehension], [Actual occasions], [Creativity]
- **Relevant Teaching:** Reality as process rather than static substances; each occasion has novel prehensions. Not explicitly reincarnation but suggests continuity of experience.
- **Reference:** Part I, Chapters 1-2

### Hartshorne - Man's Vision of God (1941)
- **Author:** Charles Hartshorne | **Period:** 20th century | **Language:** English | **Tradition:** Process Theology
- **Relevant Teaching:** Process view of divine and soul; personal identity through creative synthesis of past experiences.
- **Reference:** Chapters 1-4

## Classical & Hellenistic Philosophy

### Plato - Phaedo (ca. 380 BCE)
- **Author:** Plato | **Period:** Classical | **Language:** Ancient Greek | **Tradition:** Platonism
- **Key Concepts:** [Psyche (soul)], [Metempsychosis], [Anamnesis (recollection)]
- **Relevant Teaching:** Soul is immortal, pre-exists body, returns to world in cycles of rebirth. Argument from affinity and cyclicity.
- **Reference:** 70a-107b

### Plato - Republic Book X - Myth of Er (ca. 380 BCE)
- **Author:** Plato | **Period:** Classical | **Language:** Ancient Greek | **Tradition:** Platonism
- **Key Concepts:** [Er (mythological figure)], [Ananke (Necessity)], [Lachesis (Lot)]
- **Relevant Teaching:** Soul chooses next life; reincarnation based on moral karma; cosmic justice over many lives.
- **Reference:** Republic 614b-621d

### Plotinus - Enneads (3rd century CE)
- **Author:** Plotinus | **Period:** Late Antiquity | **Language:** Ancient Greek | **Tradition:** Neoplatonism
- **Key Concepts:** [Hypostasis (substance)], [The One], [Psyche (Soul)]
- **Relevant Teaching:** Soul descends from Intellect; falls into body; returns through virtue and philosophy. Cycle of descent and return.
- **Reference:** Ennead IV.8 (On the Descent of the Soul)

## Islamic Philosophy

### Avicenna - Kitab al-Najat (Book of Salvation, 1020s)
- **Author:** Ibn Sina (Avicenna) | **Period:** Medieval | **Language:** Arabic | **Tradition:** Aristotelian Islamic Philosophy
- **Key Concepts:** [‘Aql (Intellect)], [Nafs (Soul)], [Baqā' (Permanence)]
- **Relevant Teaching:** Rational soul is immaterial; survives death; reward/punishment in afterlife. Limited reincarnation concept.
- **Reference:** Part II, chapters on soul

### Averroes - Tahafut al-Tahafut (The Incoherence of the Incoherence, 1194)
- **Author:** Ibn Rushd (Averroes) | **Period:** Medieval | **Language:** Arabic | **Tradition:** Aristotelian Islamic Philosophy
- **Relevant Teaching:** Defense of Aristotelian view of soul; debate with Al-Ghazali on mortality vs. immortality of soul.
- **Reference:** 13th Discussion

## Christian Mysticism

### Meister Eckhart - German Sermons (14th century)
- **Author:** Meister Eckhart | **Period:** Medieval | **Language:** German | **Tradition:** Christian Mysticism
- **Key Concepts:** [Abgeschiedenheit (Detachment)], [Gelassenheit (Letting-go)], [Born des Seins (Birth of the Son)]
- **Relevant Teaching:** Soul returns to God through detachment; union with divine; no reincarnation in Christian sense but some parallels to Buddhist no-self.
- **Reference:** Sermon 6 (The Detached Life)

### Jakob Böhme - Aurora (1612)
- **Author:** Jakob Böhme | **Period:** Early Modern | **Language:** German | **Tradition:** Christian Mysticism / Theosophy
- **Key Concepts:** [Ungrund (Unground)], [Zimzum (Contraction)], [Nature]
- **Relevant Teaching:** Soul's journey through stages; emergence from God; return. Some Germanic esoteric traditions used this for reincarnation ideas.
- **Reference:** Chapters 1-7

## Psychology & Psychoanalysis

### Jung - Archetypes and the Collective Unconscious (1934)
- **Author:** Carl Gustav Jung | **Period:** 20th century | **Language:** German | **Tradition:** Analytical Psychology
- **Key Concepts:** [Archetyp], [Collectives Unbewusstes], [Individuation]
- **Relevant Teaching:** Collective unconscious contains patterns that manifest as death-rebirth motifs across cultures; individuation process includes confrontation with death.
- **Reference:** Part I, Chapter 1

### Ernest Becker - The Denial of Death (1973)
- **Author:** Ernest Becker | **Period:** 20th century | **Language:** English | **Tradition:** Existential Psychology / Terror Management
- **Key Concepts:** [Denial of death], [Heroism], [Immortality systems]
- **Relevant Teaching:** Human behavior driven by terror of mortality; culture provides symbolic immortality; reincarnation as one form.
- **Reference:** Chapters 1-4

## Scientific Research

- **Author:** Pim van Lommel et al. | **Year:** 2001 | **Journal:** The Lancet | **Methodology:** Prospective study of 344 cardiac arrest survivors
- **Key Findings:** 18% reported NDE; increased frequency with longer CPR; no correlation with oxygen levels; experiences correlated with depth of unconsciousness
- **Academic Standing:** [widely discussed / contested methodology / peer-reviewed]

- **Author:** Sam Parnia et al. | **Year:** 2014 | **Journal:** Resuscitation | **Methodology:** Prospective study of 2060 cardiac arrest patients
- **Key Findings:** 2% reported awareness during CPR; veridical perception claims; no robust evidence for out-of-body veridical perception
- **Academic Standing:** [preliminary / contested / peer-reviewed]

- **Author:** David Chalmers | **Year:** 1995 | **Journal:** Journal of Consciousness Studies | **Methodology:** Philosophical analysis
- **Key Findings:** "Hard problem" of consciousness; explanatory gap between physical processes and subjective experience; panpsychism as possible solution
