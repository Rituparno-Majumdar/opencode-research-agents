# Buddhism

Buddhism, founded by the Buddha (Siddhārtha Gautama, c. 5th century BCE), presents a distinctive doctrine of rebirth without soul (anattā). The Buddhist framework replaces the Hindu ātman with paṭiccasamuppāda (dependent origination)—a causal process where consciousness continues through the chain of dependent arising, but no substantial self persists.

## Cross-Tradition Notes
The Buddhist rejection of ātman marks the fundamental ontological divergence from Hindu thought on rebirth. Where Hinduism preserves eternal self, Buddhism insists on anattā—this is not semantic difference but irreconcilable metaphysics.
