# Śaṅkara

Śaṅkara (788–820 CE), also known as Śaṅkarācārya, was the founder of Advaita Vedānta, the non-dual school of Hindu philosophy. His doctrine of māyā and avidyā recontextualizes reincarnation: what appears as "rebirth" is actually the continuation of ignorance within the apparent individual—when avidyā is destroyed through knowledge of the Self, no further birth occurs.

## Cross-Tradition Notes
Śaṅkara's Advaita represents the most radical reconceptualization of rebirth: there is no actual transmigration—only the illusion of multiplicity continues until knowledge reveals the non-dual reality.
