## Vedic & Upanishadic Sources
### चेतनान्वय (Cetanānvaya) — Consciousness Continuity
- **Tradition:** Vedanta | **Period:** 800-400 BCE | **Language:** Sanskrit | **School:** Advaita
- **Relevant Teaching:** The Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad (1.4.10) states "aṭṭaiva somya sandhāyaitāvānāṃ nāma" — "Having laid aside ignorance, one becomes blessed." Consciousness (cit) is identified with Brahman (brahman), not reducible to matter.
- **Key Terms:** चित् (cit) — consciousness; आत्मन् (ātman) — self
- **Reference:** Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 1.4.10

### आत्मतत्त्व (Ātmatattvam) — Self-Realization
- **Tradition:** Advaita Vedanta | **Period:** 8th century CE | **Language:** Sanskrit | **School:** Advaita
- **Relevant Teaching:** Śaṅkara's Vivaruṇa presents consciousness as the self-evident foundation (svaprakāśa) of all knowledge. It cannot be objectified without presupposing itself.
- **Key Terms:** अपरोक्षानुभूति (aprakṛṣṭā anubhūti) — direct experience
- **Reference:** Brahmasūtra-bhāṣya 1.1.4

## Philosophical Schools
### साङ्ख्य (Sāṅkhya) — Twenty-Five Tattvas
- **Tradition:** Sāṅkhya | **Period:** 400 BCE - 400 CE | **Language:** Sanskrit
- **Relevant Teaching:** The Sāṅkhya system distinguishes puruṣa (consciousness) from prakṛti (matter). The twenty-three tattvas (principles) include buddhi (intellect), ahaṃkāra (ego), and manas (mind) — all material, not conscious. Only puruṣa is truly conscious.
- **Key Terms:** पुरुष (puruṣa) — pure consciousness; प्रकृति (prakṛti) — primordial matter; बुद्धि (buddhi) — intellect

### योग (Yoga) — Cittavṛtti
- **Tradition:** Yoga (Pātañjala) | **Period:** 400-200 BCE | **Language:** Sanskrit
- **Relevant Teaching:** Patanjali's Yoga Sūtras describe the modifications of consciousness (cittavṛtti). The five types of vṛttis include nirodha (cessation). The observer (drasṛ) is distinct from the observed (drśya).
- **Key Terms:** चित्तवृत्ति (cittavṛtti) — modifications of mind; निरोध (nirodha) — cessation; द्रष्टृ (drasṛ) — observer

## Buddhist Consciousness
### विज्ञान (Vijñāna) — Consciousness Only
- **Tradition:** Yogācāra/Vijñānavāda | **Period:** 4th-5th century CE | **Language:** Sanskrit/Pali
- **Relevant Teaching:** The Yogācāra school, particularly Vasubandhi's Treasury of Abhidharma, presents eight consciousnesses (aṣṭavijñāna). The eighth (ālayavijñāna) is the storehouse consciousness, continuously accumulating karmic imprints.
- **Key Terms:** विज्ञान (vijñāna) — consciousness; आलयविज्ञान (ālayavijñāna) — storehouse consciousness; अलम्बर (alambana) — object

### अनात्मवाद (Anātmavāda) — No-Self
- **Tradition:** Theravāda | **Period:** 3rd century BCE | **Language:** Pali
- **Relevant Teaching:** The doctrine of non-self (anattā) in the Pali Canon denies any permanent, unchanging self. What appears as "self" is merely the process of five aggregates (pañcakkhandha) in constant flux.
- **Key Terms:** अनात्मन् (anattā) — no-self; पञ्चस्कन्ध (pañcaskandha) — five aggregates; उपादान (upādāna) — clinging

## Tantric & Consciousness Models
### महासुख (Mahāsukha) — Great Bliss
- **Tradition:** Vajrayāna/Kashmir Shaivism | **Period:** 9th-11th century CE | **Language:** Sanskrit
- **Relevant Teaching:** The Spanda-Kārikā by Kṣemaraja describes consciousness (spanda) as the primordial vibration. The ground of being is not static but dynamically conscious.
- **Key Terms:** स्पन्द (spanda) — vibration/pulse; परमशिव (paramashiva) — supreme Shiva; विमर्श (vimarśa) — self-reflective awareness
